## The 10th and 4th House Integration

I finished reading *Yesteryear* by Caro Claire Burke.

It is a satirical novel about Natalie Heller Mills, a tradwife influencer whose public life is built around an idealized vision of traditional family, beauty, domesticity, and success. Then she wakes up in 1855 and is forced to confront the reality of the world she had been romanticizing and monetizing online. The book is about much more than influencer culture, but what stayed with me most was the psychological cost of digital validation. Which, to me, is really the cost of validation itself.

I do not want to be an influencer. I use social media because I hope it can help me build an astrology practice with around ten clients a week. But the machinery is the same. Social media constantly asks us to turn a human life into a public interface.

That interface can project wellness, beauty, luxury, success, spirituality, intelligence, stability, perfect relationships, perfect families. Behind it, the person may be exhausted, insecure, traumatized, anxious, disordered, lonely, or simply living a very ordinary human life that does not match the image. The pressure becomes even more intense when income depends on remaining visible. Post every day. Stay relevant. Feed the algorithm. Watch the likes, views, shares, follows, and unfollows. Emotional stability begins to rise and fall with metrics that are fundamentally unpredictable.

For family creators, there is another layer. Children can become part of the product before they are old enough to understand what privacy means. The boundary between intimate life and public material can disappear very early.

To me, the influencer landscape shows a psychological crisis in concentrated form. When human worth becomes digital currency, the pressure to perform can fracture the relationship between the person who is living and the persona who is being rewarded. But this is not something that belongs only to influencers. Influencer culture is society on steroids. It takes something deeply human and turns the volume all the way up.

A lot of us will never allow the parts we do not like about ourselves to be seen. Creators are dealing with the same problem, only at higher intensity. The optimized public image can become very different from the interior self, and then the person becomes dependent on exterior validation to maintain a persona that may no longer feel like home.

As an example, the more I defend radical authenticity, the more I sometimes get rejected by people who do not want to look at the not-so-graceful aspects of being human. When you work with the public, rejection has material consequences. If people reject you, they may also not hire you. That makes public visibility a terrifyingly efficient machine for shadow creation. You begin to learn which parts of yourself are rewarded, which are ignored, and which cost you approval.

In astrological terms, I understand this through the 4th and 10th Houses. Here we find the tension between public space and intimate space, between what is visible and what is rooted underneath. They are always operating along a gradient. Sometimes we live with more privacy, sometimes with more exposure. Sometimes we allow a fuller expression of being, and sometimes we hide in the corners of ourselves and remain silent.

The 10th House describes public life, reputation, visibility, and the ways we are recognized by the world. With a great deal of emphasis there, a person can develop a very strong exterior identity. That identity may become so developed that the quieter internal voice is barely recognizable. Some people call that voice the soul. Some call it instinct. Some simply call it the self that exists when nobody is watching.

When the public identity takes over, fragmentation can happen. The real emotional needs of the person, which I associate with the 4th House, can be neglected, minimized, or treated as inconvenient. The integration of the being requires that the 4th House and the 10th House check and balance each other, so the root of the person can express itself publicly without being sacrificed to the public image.

This book shows what happens when society develops an immensely packed 10th House and allows it to take over the 4th.

I strongly believe we have to fight for the health of that 4th House. Nurse that need. Hear that need. Connect with it. That need is connected with the craving of the soul, with whatever mysterious force gathered enough material to make you become you. My own metaphysical imagination sees rebirth as matter and energy coming together through commonality, creating an idea, and from that idea a being materializing in the head of the Goddess, who gives us the body through which we can exist. Whether anyone shares that image or not, I think we still have to find the real craving for life underneath performance and nurse it.

I may communicate too painfully openly about my feelings, but I find that this is how I see them best. I see them echoing in the material world. I observe the energies they attract and the energies they repel.

One observation I keep making, without pretending it is universal, is that some introverted people seem especially uncomfortable with the exposure of raw emotional matter. I do not dare to explain why. I simply observe it. Healers, on the other hand, often seem attracted to that expression and embrace me through it. The empath team. Without them, how would we exist?

I also notice that some highly intellectual people are uncomfortable with expression that contains too much animal in it. The thinking self can imagine itself as belonging to a more evolved class of being, and then the animal material becomes offensive. The reaction I receive from many men to that rawness often feels sexual. They feel it in their animal too, and our culture has delegated so much of the animal within us to sexuality alone.

The more cerebral we become, the more tempted we may be to treat sexual urges as impure. The more devoted we are to spiritual systems built around regulation, the more we may try to discipline that force into something acceptable. But the truth is that, in this body, you are an animal. And when you die, you do not actually know what is going to happen. Faith is not knowing.

So the soul material, the animal material, the emotional material, everything underneath the shallow aspects of being, can be pushed underground by shame. Compared with the 10th House promise of *I am validated by the love of millions*, the inner voice can seem too small to matter.

But that almost silent voice is extraordinarily powerful.

It can break you to live too far away from it.

It does not matter how much you regulate.

*Yesteryear* is a great book. You should read it.
